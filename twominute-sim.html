Two-Minute Drill

Each drive starts with a random amount of time (anywhere from 0:10 to 2:00) and a random number of timeouts (1 to 3), with the ball at your own 25. Drive the field and score. Every snap has a random play call, a random coverage, a full four-man pass rush and a random blitz of 0 to 3 extra rushers. Before each snap you can see the play call, its routes and the defense lined up. The coverage is never labeled. Read the alignment yourself and find out what it was after the play. Throw to the first open receiver, or throw it away if nobody is open. After a catch the receiver runs until a defender touches him, and then he is down. Running out of bounds or an incomplete pass stops the clock. A catch in bounds does not, so use a timeout or spike to save time. Your passing stats from each drive (completions, attempts, yards, touchdowns and interceptions) give you an NFL passer rating.

Session setup

Level: 1/10

The starting clock (0:10 to 2:00) and the number of timeouts (1 to 3) are picked at random for every drive. Play calls and coverages are random every snap. The pass rush is always full and blitzes are random. Level 10 = real pass-rush timing. Levels 1-9 add training time. More blitzers shorten the pressure window. A snap with the clock running costs 10 seconds. A timeout stops that clock for free. A spike costs 2 seconds and a down. The drive ends on a touchdown, a made or missed field goal, an interception, a turnover on downs or when time runs out. Your score is saved to the Progress tab. If players have been added to the Players tab, you can pick one to save to that player instead.

Coaching cues
  • Look at the safeties first. Two deep safeties often mean Cover 2 or man. One deep safety often means Cover 1 or Cover 3. No deep safety means Cover 0 and a blitz.
  • Defenders close to the line over your receivers usually mean man coverage.
  • Sidelines and incompletions stop the clock. Middle-of-the-field catches do not.
  • With the clock running and under about 30 seconds, call a timeout or spike before you snap.
  • Throwing it away is a good play. It stops the clock and keeps the drive alive.
  • Do not force it. An interception ends the drive.
