4/5 WR Concepts

Use these concepts when the defense is spread out and the quarterback must make fast, clock-aware decisions. In a two-minute situation, the goal is to move the chains, protect the ball, and use the sideline or an incomplete pass to stop the clock.

4-WR Concepts

Spread / 2x2

Two receivers on each side. Good for quick reads, flats, curls, slants, and sideline outs.

Trips

Three receivers to one side and one isolated receiver to the other. Creates spacing advantages and forces the defense to declare its coverage.

Bunch

Three receivers close together with one isolated. Great for quick game, pick concepts, and easy throws against man coverage.

Empty (4 WR)

No running back in the backfield. The quarterback must recognize pressure quickly and use hot throws or throwaways.

Mesh

Two shallow crossers create a high-low read. Best against man coverage; find the first open crosser and throw quickly.

Levels

One shallow route and one intermediate route in the same area. Read shallow to deep and attack the open level.

Spacing

Three short, easy throws spread across the field. Use it to keep the chains moving and avoid forcing deep throws.

Smash

A corner route and a hitch route to the same side. Attack the deep defender’s leverage; take the hitch if he sits.

5-WR Concepts

Empty / 5 Eligible

All five eligible receivers are spread out. There is no running back protection help, so identify the blitz before the snap.

5 Vertical

Five receivers attack different depths. Use it to stress zone coverage, but do not force a deep throw if the first read is covered.

Quick Game

Slants, flats, quick outs, and bubble screens. These are ideal when the defense is blitzing and the quarterback has little time.

RPO

Read an underneath defender after the snap. If he attacks a receiver, throw the quick route; if he stays, hand off or keep the ball.

Sight Adjustment

If the defense blitzes, a receiver may change his route into a hot throw. Know the pre-snap rule before the snap.

Scramble Rules

When pressure breaks the pocket, receivers should work to an open area and get vertical. The quarterback should throw away rather than force a covered throw.

QB Reads

  • Pre-snap: Count the rushers, find the safeties, and identify whether the defense looks like man or zone.
  • Man coverage: Use crossing routes, mesh, spacing, quick outs, and scrambles.
  • Zone coverage: Find the open grass, throw between zones, and attack the soft spots in the flats or middle.
  • Blitz: Get to the hot route quickly. If there is no open hot throw, throw it away.
  • Progression: Read one side first, then the other. Do not stare down one receiver.
  • Decision rule: Throw to the first open receiver. If nobody is open, throw it away and play the next down.

Clock Management

  • An incomplete pass stops the clock.
  • A catch near the sideline can stop the clock if the receiver gets out of bounds.
  • A catch in the middle keeps the clock running, so use a timeout or spike when needed.
  • Under 30 seconds with the clock running, call a timeout or spike before snapping.
  • Do not take a deep shot unless it is open and the clock allows it.
  • Protect the football: an interception ends the drive.

Coaching Cues

  • 4-WR: spread the field, find the first open receiver, and use quick throws.
  • 5-WR: recognize pressure early, know the hot route, and do not hold the ball.
  • Against man, use crossing routes and spacing.
  • Against zone, throw into open grass between defenders.
  • Use the sideline when you need to stop the clock.
  • Take the easy completion to stay on schedule.
