Game Situations

Every snap uses 4 or 5 eligible receivers. Receiver #3 is the running back, aligned left or right of the quarterback, with RB routes that break to his side.

Session setup

Level: 1/10

Formations include 2x2, trips, bunch, and empty looks. Concepts include slants, flood, levels, verticals, mesh, spacing, smash, dagger, curl-flat, stick, Y-cross, China, and shallow cross. The running back can run a swing, flat, angle, wheel, or stay in pass protection. Defensive looks include Cover 2 Man, Cover 3 Match, Cover 6, Cover 4 Palms, fire zones, and simulated blitzes.

Coaching cues
  • Read the safeties first: two deep often means Cover 2, Cover 4, or man; one deep often means Cover 1, Cover 3, or a fire zone; no deep safety means Cover 0.
  • Defenders close to receivers usually mean man coverage.
  • Cover 6 has one deep safety and one deep corner — read the half side.
  • Cover 4 Palms: corners and safeties match outside and slot routes.
  • Fire zones bring five but drop three underneath — get rid of it fast.
  • Simulated blitzes show pressure but some defenders drop — read the real rush, not the look.
  • The running back’s route breaks toward the side he is aligned on.
  • Sideline catches and incompletions stop the clock; middle-of-field catches do not.
  • Under 30 seconds with the clock running, use a timeout or spike.
  • Do not force throws into coverage—throw it away if nobody is open.
